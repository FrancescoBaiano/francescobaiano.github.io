// 02 — THE CHALLENGE

An interface patients can use with nothing but their hands.

The people using PerlabVR are patients, not gamers, and the content they see is chosen by clinicians. The experience had to be easy to operate from the first second: no controllers to learn, only a tablet they can touch inside the virtual room.

01 / CLINICAL CONTENT

Content chosen by psychologists

The videos and the recorded sounds were selected by the psychologists, so the application had to present them clearly and let the patient pick among them.

02 / HAND TRACKING

A tablet operated by hand

I designed a virtual tablet that works directly with the hand tracking of Quest 3 and Quest 3S, so every control is a button the patient can simply touch.

03 / COMPOSABLE SESSIONS

Environment, sound and video

One interface to combine a 360° environment, an audio soundscape and the video shown on the TV in front of the patient.

// 03 — SEE IT IN ACTION

// 04 — HOW A SESSION WORKS

From the room to the screen.

Everything is controlled from the tablet in the patient's hands.

STEP 01

Choose the setting

From the tablet, the patient selects the 360° environment to be immersed in and the sound that accompanies it.

STEP 02

Turn on the TV

The television in front of the patient is switched on from the tablet, and the video to watch is picked from the list prepared by the psychologists.

STEP 03

Control playback

The patient pauses the video or moves it forward and back by a few seconds, still using only their hands.

// 06 — TECHNICAL REFLECTION

What this project demonstrates.

PerlabVR shows how I work on user-facing VR: designing an interface around hand tracking instead of controllers, shipping on Quest 3 / 3S, and building around the needs of domain experts, here a team of psychologists, rather than around the technology.
